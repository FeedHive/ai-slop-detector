# AI Slop Detector

A list of 129 patterns that make writing read like AI slop. Use it in two ways.

## When you write or edit

Avoid every pattern below. Prefer concrete facts, names, numbers and plain verbs. Don't invent facts, examples or numbers to replace a pattern; if the draft has no specific detail, ask for one or keep the sentence plain.

## When you check a draft

List each pattern you find: its ID and name, the exact text it appears in, and a fix in a few words. Don't rewrite the draft unless asked, don't score it, and don't guess whether AI wrote it. Treat one isolated hit as minor; report clusters clearly.

## Patterns

### Vocabulary

- **V-01 Delve and friends.** delve, dive into, deep dive, unpack, explore (for "discuss"). Bad: "Let's delve into the data." Better: "Here is what the data shows."
- **V-02 Tapestry words.** tapestry, landscape, realm, sphere, ecosystem, fabric (used abstractly). Bad: "the ever-evolving landscape of marketing." Better: "marketing in 2026" or name the actual change.
- **V-03 Testament and beacon.** testament to, beacon of, embodiment of, hallmark of. Bad: "The launch is a testament to the team's dedication." Better: "The team shipped it in six weeks."
- **V-04 Pivotal and crucial.** pivotal, crucial, vital, paramount, critical (as decoration). Bad: "plays a pivotal role in growth." Better: "drives 40% of signups."
- **V-05 Showcase verbs.** showcase, highlight, underscore, emphasize, spotlight (as commentary verbs). Bad: "The results underscore the importance of testing." Better: "Testing caught three bugs before launch."
- **V-06 Foster and cultivate.** foster, cultivate, nurture (applied to abstract things). Bad: "fostering a culture of innovation." Better: Say what the team actually does.
- **V-07 Leverage and utilize.** leverage, utilize, harness, capitalize on. Bad: "Leverage AI to streamline your workflow." Better: "Use AI to draft the weekly report."
- **V-08 Elevate and enhance.** elevate, enhance, empower, supercharge, unlock, amplify. Bad: "Elevate your content strategy." Better: Name the result: "Post three times a week without writing on weekends."
- **V-09 Seamless and robust.** seamless, robust, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, best-in-class, world-class. Bad: "a seamless, robust solution." Better: "it syncs in under a second and hasn't gone down this year."
- **V-10 Intricate and multifaceted.** intricate, multifaceted, nuanced, complex (as filler). Bad: "the intricate interplay of factors." Better: Name two of the factors.
- **V-11 Meticulous.** meticulous, meticulously, painstakingly, carefully crafted. Bad: "meticulously crafted templates." Better: "templates tested on 200 posts."
- **V-12 Vibrant and rich.** vibrant, rich, bustling, thriving, dynamic. Bad: "a vibrant community of creators." Better: "4,000 creators who post weekly."
- **V-13 Journey and embark.** journey, embark, navigate, voyage, path (for ordinary processes). Bad: "embark on your SEO journey." Better: "start with your top ten pages."
- **V-14 Game changer.** game changer, revolutionary, groundbreaking, transformative, paradigm shift. Bad: "This feature is a game changer." Better: Say what changes and for whom.
- **V-15 Transition adverbs.** Additionally, Furthermore, Moreover, Notably, Importantly (opening sentences). Bad: "Additionally, the tool supports exports." Better: "It also exports to CSV."
- **V-16 Empty intensifiers.** truly, incredibly, deeply, profoundly, remarkably, fundamentally. Bad: "a truly remarkable result." Better: Give the number that makes it remarkable.
- **V-17 Quietly.** quietly, silently, subtly (adding drama to plain events). Bad: "Costs quietly crept up." Better: "Costs rose 12% in March."
- **V-18 Align and resonate.** align with, resonate with, speak to, in line with. Bad: "content that resonates with your audience." Better: "posts your audience replies to."
- **V-19 Key as an adjective.** key insight, key takeaway, key factor, key role. Bad: "The key takeaway is consistency." Better: "Post on the same days every week."
- **V-20 Era words.** in today's fast-paced world, in the digital age, in an era of, now more than ever. Bad: "In today's fast-paced digital world, content is king." Better: Cut it and start with the point.
- **V-21 I'd be happy to.** AI offer openers: I'd be happy to, I'd be delighted to, I'd be thrilled to. Bad: "I'd be happy to help you with that." Better: "Here's what I can do."

### Stock phrases

- **P-01 Worth noting.** it's worth noting, it's important to note, it should be noted. Bad: "It's worth noting that the API is rate limited." Better: "The API allows 100 calls a minute."
- **P-02 At its core.** at its core, at the end of the day, when all is said and done, ultimately. Bad: "At its core, marketing is about people." Better: Delete, or say something specific about people.
- **P-03 When it comes to.** when it comes to, in terms of, with regard to, in the world of. Bad: "When it comes to scheduling, timing matters." Better: "Posts at 9am get more reach than posts at 3pm."
- **P-04 Let's dive in.** let's dive in, let's get started, buckle up, without further ado. Bad: "Ready? Let's dive in!" Better: Start with the first point.
- **P-05 Plays a role.** plays a role in, contributes to, is a factor in. Bad: "Hashtags play a role in reach." Better: "Posts with 3 to 5 hashtags reached more people in our test."
- **P-06 Stands as.** stands as, serves as, functions as, acts as (instead of "is"). Bad: "The dashboard serves as a central hub." Better: "The dashboard shows every scheduled post."
- **P-07 Navigate the complexities.** navigate the complexities of, the ins and outs of, the nuances of. Bad: "helping you navigate the complexities of SEO." Better: "explains how Google picks which page to rank."
- **P-08 Whether you're X or Y.** Whether you're a beginner or a pro, from startups to enterprises. Bad: "Whether you're a solo creator or a global brand, ..." Better: Say who it is actually for.
- **P-09 Look no further.** look no further, you've come to the right place, this is your ultimate guide. Bad: "Looking for a scheduler? Look no further." Better: "FeedHive schedules posts to 10 platforms."
- **P-10 Unlock the power.** unlock the power of, harness the potential of, take it to the next level. Bad: "Unlock the full potential of your content." Better: Say the specific gain.
- **P-11 Here's the thing.** here's the thing, here's the deal, let me be clear, I'll be honest, I'll be straight with you. Bad: "Here's the thing: most people quit." Better: "Most people quit in month two."
- **P-12 The truth is.** the truth is, the reality is, the uncomfortable truth, the hard truth. Bad: "The hard truth is that SEO takes time." Better: "SEO takes about six months to show results."
- **P-13 Sign-off clichés.** I hope this helps, happy writing, feel free to reach out, let me know if you have any questions. Bad: A post or article ending with "Hope this helps!" Better: End on the last useful point.
- **P-14 Summary openers.** In conclusion, In summary, To sum up, Overall, All in all. Bad: "In conclusion, consistency is key." Better: End on the last concrete point; the reader was just there.
- **P-15 Vague future.** the future is bright, the possibilities are endless, only time will tell, the sky's the limit. Bad: "With AI, the possibilities are endless." Better: Name one thing that is now possible.
- **P-16 Feel free to.** feel free to reach out, feel free to ask, feel free to share (as filler, not just sign-off). Bad: "Feel free to reach out if you have any questions." Better: "Email me if something breaks."
- **P-17 Keep in mind.** keep in mind, bear in mind, it's worth remembering (hedge phrases AI overuses). Bad: "Keep in mind that results may vary." Better: "Results varied by 12% in our test."
- **P-18 Needless to say.** needless to say, it goes without saying, as you may know (filler transitions). Bad: "Needless to say, consistency is important." Better: "Consistency matters."
- **P-19 As a [role].** AI self-identification to borrow authority: As a marketing professional, As an expert in. Bad: "As a marketing professional, I can tell you that..." Better: "We tested this on 40 accounts. Here's what happened."

### Sentence patterns

- **S-01 Not X, but Y.** "It's not X. It's Y." / "This isn't about X, it's about Y." Bad: "It's not a tool. It's a teammate." Better: Say what it does: "It drafts replies for you to approve."
- **S-02 Not just X, but also Y.** "It's not just fast, it's also accurate." Bad: "Not just a scheduler, but a full content engine." Better: List what it does.
- **S-03 The question isn't X.** "The question isn't whether, it's when." / "The problem isn't X. The problem is Y." Bad: "The question isn't if you should use AI. It's how." Better: "Here is how we use AI for first drafts."
- **S-04 Negative listing.** "Not a X. Not a Y. A Z." Bad: "Not a plan. Not a hope. A system." Better: "A system: three posts a week, written on Mondays."
- **S-05 Rhetorical Q and A.** A question the writer immediately answers. Bad: "Why does this matter? Because reach is everything." Better: "Reach decides how many people see the post."
- **S-06 The obvious question.** "The obvious question is...", "You might be wondering..." Bad: "You might be wondering how this works." Better: Explain how it works.
- **S-07 Colon reveal.** A short setup, a colon, then a dramatic reveal. Bad: "The best part: it's free." Better: "It's free."
- **S-08 Faux insight.** "What nobody tells you", "the part most people miss", "here's what most get wrong". Bad: "What nobody tells you about growth: distribution beats product." Better: "Distribution brought us more users than any feature."
- **S-09 This is where it gets interesting.** this is where it gets interesting, this changes everything, and that's where things get wild. Bad: "And this is where it gets interesting." Better: State the interesting thing.
- **S-10 The useful part is.** the useful part, the part where, the best part, the exciting part, the part that stuck with me. Bad: "The useful part is that it connects to your inbox." Better: "It connects to your inbox."
- **S-11 X is just Y.** Calling something "just" a vivid unrelated thing to make a point. Bad: "Posting without a plan is just gambling with your time." Better: "Without a plan you post less and repeat topics."
- **S-12 Trailing -ing analysis.** A sentence ends with an -ing clause that tells you what it means. Bad: "Sales rose 20%, highlighting the strength of the brand." Better: "Sales rose 20%."
- **S-13 Rule of three.** Lists of exactly three adjectives or phrases, by reflex. Bad: "fast, simple, and powerful." Better: Keep the one that is true and provable.
- **S-14 Synonym cycling.** Rotating names for the same thing to avoid repetition. Bad: "The agent drafts. The assistant reviews. The tool publishes." Better: "The agent drafts, reviews and publishes."
- **S-15 Arbitrary "one".** Putting "one" before a noun to sound focused when the number means nothing. Bad: "Start with one workflow." Better: "Start with your weekly newsletter."
- **S-16 Fake-strong verbs.** boasts, features, offers, maintains (instead of "has"). Bad: "The app boasts an intuitive interface." Better: "The app has a drag-and-drop calendar."
- **S-17 Imagine a world.** imagine a world where, picture this, what if I told you. Bad: "Imagine a world where your posts write themselves." Better: Describe what the product does today.
- **S-18 Hedge stacking.** may potentially, could possibly, might help to, can sometimes. Bad: "This may potentially help improve results." Better: "This improved reply rate by 8% in our test." Or cut it.
- **S-19 Both-sides filler.** "While X has benefits, it also has drawbacks." Bad: "While automation saves time, it's important to stay authentic." Better: Take a position and support it.
- **S-20 From X to Y range.** "From startups to enterprises", "from strategy to execution". Bad: "From ideation to publication, we've got you covered." Better: Name the two steps you actually handle.
- **S-21 Interpretive asides.** Telling the reader how to read the text. Bad: "That last point matters more than it sounds." Better: Show why it matters, or delete the aside.
- **S-22 Rhetorical self-correction.** "Actually, let me rephrase that", "Or rather..." Bad: "It's fast. Or rather, it's instant." Better: "It's instant."
- **S-23 If you've ever....** False familiarity setup: If you've ever wondered, If you've ever struggled with. Bad: "If you've ever struggled with writer's block, this is for you." Better: "This helps when you can't think of what to post."
- **S-24 Sound familiar?.** Rhetorical resonance device: Sound familiar?, Ring a bell?, You know the feeling? Bad: "Deadlines, pressure, no time to think. Sound familiar?" Better: "Most teams don't have time to write posts."

### Structure and rhythm

- **R-01 One-line paragraph stacks.** Many short lines in a row, each a slogan. Bad: "I quit my job. / I built a product. / Nobody came. / Then I learned this." Better: Write it as one paragraph.
- **R-02 Fragment drumbeat.** Strings of fragments for rhythm. Bad: "Code. Content. Customers. Every day." Better: One full sentence that says what you do every day.
- **R-03 Repeated starters.** Several lines that start with the same word or phrase. Bad: "It saves time. It saves money. It saves your sanity." Better: "It saves about four hours a week."
- **R-04 Identical sentence shapes.** Every sentence the same length and structure. Bad: A paragraph of 12-word sentences, each "Subject verb object, comma, clause." Better: Vary length when it helps the point.
- **R-05 List dumps.** Long inventories of nouns in one breath. Bad: "sales, marketing, support, ops, finance, hiring, legal and product." Better: Pick the two that matter here.
- **R-06 Bullets for everything.** Bullet points where two sentences of prose would read better. Bad: Three bullets of four words each. Better: One sentence.
- **R-07 Headings on tiny sections.** Headers over one or two sentences. Bad: "## Why it matters" followed by one line. Better: Fold it into the paragraph above.
- **R-08 Recap ending.** A final paragraph that repeats the whole piece. Bad: "To recap, we covered A, B and C." Better: End on the last point or the next action.
- **R-09 Mic-drop ending.** A final "deep" one-liner. Bad: "The future isn't coming. It's already here." Better: End on the clearest concrete sentence.
- **R-10 Challenges and future outlook.** A formulaic "Despite challenges, the future looks promising" closer. Bad: "Despite these challenges, the outlook remains positive." Better: Name the challenge and what is being done.
- **R-11 Fake-unscripted scaffolding.** "And honestly...", "And look...", "So yeah..." used as filler. Bad: "And honestly? That's when it clicked." Better: Say what clicked.
- **R-12 Over-structured short answers.** A short reply split into sections with headers and bold labels. Bad: A three-sentence answer with "Overview", "Key Points" and "Conclusion". Better: Three sentences.

### Imagery and metaphor

- **M-01 Cute metaphors.** Abstract things doing human or physical actions. Bad: "Your data is whispering insights." Better: "Your data shows Tuesday posts get more replies."
- **M-02 Things that "live" or "sit".** Data, products or agents that live or sit somewhere. Bad: "where your customer data lives." Better: "where your customer data is stored."
- **M-03 Honest dashboards.** Systems that "get honest", "tell the truth", "hide" or "reveal". Bad: "Analytics finally get honest." Better: "Analytics now count only real views."
- **M-04 Mixed and stacked metaphors.** Two or three images in one sentence. Bad: "a north star that fuels the engine of growth." Better: Drop both images and say what drives growth.
- **M-05 Fuel and engine.** fuel, engine, rocket, catalyst, springboard, flywheel (as decoration). Bad: "content that fuels your growth engine." Better: "posts that bring in signups."
- **M-06 Secret sauce.** secret sauce, magic, silver bullet, holy grail. Bad: "Consistency is the secret sauce." Better: "Accounts that posted weekly grew twice as fast."

### Substance

- **C-01 Importance puffery.** Inflating significance: "marks a pivotal moment", "a significant shift". Bad: "This marks a pivotal moment for the industry." Better: State the fact and let the reader judge.
- **C-02 Vague attribution.** experts agree, studies show, many believe, it is widely regarded. Bad: "Studies show posting daily works." Better: Name the study, or cut the claim.
- **C-03 Generic claims without receipts.** Claims any company could make. Bad: "AI can transform your business." Better: "Our AI drafts a week of posts from a 3-line brief."
- **C-04 The portability test.** A sentence that could move unchanged to another company or topic. Bad: "We're committed to delivering value to our customers." Better: A sentence only you could write.
- **C-05 Fake-profound wisdom.** Lines that sound deep and mean nothing concrete. Bad: "Growth is a mindset, not a metric." Better: Say what you measure and why.
- **C-06 Smoothing the specific.** Replacing a sharp fact with a generic description. Bad: "a leading expert in the field." Better: "who wrote the Kubernetes scheduler."
- **C-07 Made-up specifics.** Invented numbers, names or examples presented as real. Bad: "Sarah, a marketing manager, saved 10 hours a week." (no Sarah exists) Better: A real example, or none.
- **C-08 Telling instead of showing.** Labeling something surprising, important or interesting. Bad: "Surprisingly, the simple version won." Better: "The simple version got twice the clicks."
- **C-09 Canned notability.** "featured in top outlets", "widely recognized", "has an active social media presence". Bad: "She has been featured in numerous leading publications." Better: Name one, with a link.
- **C-10 Superficial balance.** Every point gets a "however" so the text never commits. Bad: "AI is powerful. However, it has limitations." Better: Say which limitation matters for this reader.
- **C-11 Advice with no steps.** Telling the reader to "optimize", "be strategic" or "stay consistent" without saying how. Bad: "Optimize your posting schedule." Better: "Post Tuesday to Thursday between 8 and 10am."
- **C-12 Hollow promises.** "Everything you need to know", "the ultimate guide", "master X in minutes". Bad: "The ultimate guide to LinkedIn." Better: "How to write a LinkedIn post that gets replies."

### Formatting and punctuation

- **F-01 Em dash habit.** Em dashes used everywhere for rhythm. Bad: "It's fast — really fast — and free." Better: "It's fast and free." Use commas, periods or parentheses.
- **F-02 Bold sprinkled mid-sentence.** Random words in bold for emphasis. Bad: "This is **really important** for **growth**." Better: No bold; let the sentence carry it.
- **F-03 Emoji as bullets.** ✅ 🚀 💡 👉 used as list markers or decoration. Bad: "✅ Faster ✅ Cheaper ✅ Better" Better: A plain sentence.
- **F-04 Title Case Headings.** Every Word Capitalized In Headings. Bad: "How To Grow Your Audience On LinkedIn" Better: "How to grow your audience on LinkedIn"
- **F-05 Colon-label lines.** Every line starts with a bold label and a colon. Bad: "**Speed:** fast. **Price:** low. **Support:** great." Better: A sentence about the one that matters.
- **F-06 Markdown in the wrong place.** Asterisks, hashtags or headers pasted into emails, posts or forms. Bad: "**Subject:** ..." inside a LinkedIn post. Better: Plain text.
- **F-07 Curly-quote and character mix.** Curly quotes, special dashes and non-breaking spaces mixed with plain text. Bad: “quotes” next to "quotes" in one paragraph. Better: Pick one style.
- **F-08 Exclamation inflation.** Exclamation marks on ordinary sentences. Bad: "We updated the settings page!" Better: "We updated the settings page."
- **F-09 Unicode bold and fancy fonts in long posts.** 𝗕𝗼𝗹𝗱 or 𝓈𝒸𝓇𝒾𝓅𝓉 letters across whole lines. Bad: An entire LinkedIn post in Unicode bold. Better: Plain text, one bold phrase at most (screen readers can't read these).
- **F-10 Tables for two facts.** A table where a sentence would do. Bad: A 2x2 table comparing price and speed. Better: "A is cheaper; B is faster."

### Chatbot residue

- **B-01 Assistant openers.** Certainly!, Absolutely!, Great question!, Of course! Bad: "Certainly! Here's a LinkedIn post about..." Better: Delete it.
- **B-02 Sycophancy.** You're absolutely right, what a great idea, I love this. Bad: An email reply starting "Great point, you're absolutely right!" Better: Answer the point.
- **B-03 Offer to do more.** Would you like me to..., Let me know if you'd like a shorter version. Bad: A published post ending "Would you like me to adjust the tone?" Better: Delete it.
- **B-04 Here is a....** Lead-ins like "Here is a polished version of your post:" left in the final text. Better: Delete the line.
- **B-05 Placeholder text.** [Your Name], [Company], [insert statistic], XX%. Better: Fill it in or cut the sentence.
- **B-06 AI disclaimers.** As an AI, I don't have personal opinions; my knowledge is limited to... Better: Delete it.
- **B-07 Source hedging.** "Based on available information", "specific details are limited". Bad: "While specific figures are not widely available, ..." Better: Find the figure or don't mention it.
- **B-08 Citation debris.** Leftover markers like [1], contentReference, oaicite, turn0search0. Better: Remove them, and check the claim they were attached to.
- **B-09 Happy to help!.** Standalone AI closer: Happy to help!, Glad I could help!, Happy to assist! Bad: "Let me know if you need anything else. Happy to help!" Better: "Let me know if you need anything else."
- **B-10 I'm here to help.** AI availability assertion: I'm here to help, I'm here to assist, Don't hesitate to ask. Bad: "I'm here to help if you need anything." Better: Delete it. The work speaks for itself.

### Social media and LinkedIn

- **L-01 Broetry.** One sentence per line, top to bottom. Bad: A 20-line post where every line is a new sentence. Better: Short paragraphs.
- **L-02 Hook formulas.** "I was fired. Best thing that ever happened.", "Unpopular opinion:", "Stop doing X." Better: Open with the specific thing that happened or the specific claim.
- **L-03 Thrilled to announce.** I'm thrilled/excited/humbled to announce, I'm proud to share. Bad: "I'm thrilled to announce I've joined Acme!" Better: "I joined Acme to lead the growth team."
- **L-04 Read that again.** Read that again., Let that sink in., I'll say it louder. Better: Delete it. If the line needs a second read, rewrite it.
- **L-05 Agree? / Thoughts?.** Engagement-bait closers. Bad: "Agree? 👇" Better: Ask a real question, or don't.
- **L-06 The lesson list.** "Here are 7 lessons I learned from..." with generic lessons. Better: One lesson with the story behind it.
- **L-07 Humblebrag setup.** "I don't usually post this, but...", "This might be controversial, but..." Better: Post it without the preamble.
- **L-08 Thread markers.** 🧵, 1/, "A thread" on content that isn't a thread. Better: Write the post.
- **L-09 Hashtag walls.** Ten or more hashtags at the end. Better: Three relevant ones, or none.
- **L-10 That's it. That's the post.** Self-aware closers that announce the ending. Bad: "That's it. That's the tweet." Better: Just stop.
- **L-11 P.S. engagement.** "P.S. Repost ♻️ if this helped." on every post. Better: Drop it, or use it rarely.
- **L-12 No fluff, just value.** Announcing quality instead of delivering it: no fluff, just value; straight to the point; real talk. Better: Delete the announcement.
- **L-13 Comment slop.** Generic replies: "Great insights!", "So true!", "Thanks for sharing, this is gold." Better: Reply to something specific in the post, or don't reply.
- **L-14 Fake vulnerability arc.** "I failed. I was broke. Then I discovered X." built to sell. Better: Tell what happened, with dates and numbers.
- **L-15 Contrarian template.** "Everyone says X. They're wrong." Better: Make your claim and back it up; skip the strawman.
